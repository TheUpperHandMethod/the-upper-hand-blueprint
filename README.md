## The premise

Every year, abstract reasoning gets cheaper — drafting, summarizing, planning, analyzing is becoming a utility. When a capability becomes cheap, it stops being an advantage. What stays scarce: **hands** (physical, in-the-world competence), **reliability** (being the version of a person who finishes), and **judgment with a system under it**. An AI assistant strengthens none of these by itself; left unstructured, it can quietly weaken all three — it remembers for you until you stop checking, and agrees fluently until you stop deciding.

This blueprint is the system that goes under the judgment: five written mechanisms, plus one growth loop, for running a human + AI partnership in which every important claim is traceable, every important decision is made before the pressure arrives, and every error gets converted into a rule instead of a regret.

One honest sentence, because honesty is the entire brand: **this method does not make a human + AI pair automatically better than either one alone.** The best controlled evidence says that, on average, they are not (Vaccaro et al. 2024, *Nature Human Behaviour*, 106 experiments: human + AI combinations performed worse than the better of the two alone, g ≈ −0.23). What the method does is narrower: it makes the pair's performance *measurable*, its errors *findable*, and its lessons *permanent*.

## The five mechanisms

1. **Pre-commitments with falsifiers** — before any decision that matters, write what you decided, what you expect, your confidence, and the specific evidence that would prove you wrong. A commitment without a falsifier is just a hope with a date on it.
2. **Graded ledgers** — every checkable call is logged when it is made, with confidence and falsifier attached, then graded on its resolution date: RIGHT, WRONG, or TOO EARLY. Memory is a public-relations firm for its owner; the ledger is memory that cannot be lobbied. The ledger is primary — when a copy and the ledger disagree, the ledger wins.
3. **Proof dates** — every mechanism or routine you adopt ships with a named date and the test that will prove it works. Unproven past its date is a gap, not a success — logged in a gap register with a closing condition.
4. **Receipts for state changes** — every state change gets a dated receipt written at the moment of the change, and every claim about current state carries its evidence class: **landed** (read just now from the surface where the state lives), **reported** (a record says so), or **inferred** (computed, not observed). When a fact changes, every surface that carries it is updated in the same pass.
5. **Restore drills** — periodically, export the whole system as plain files and hand them, alone, to a cold reader (a second AI engine, or a trusted person) to reconstruct your state from the files only. Grade the reconstruction. Plant a canary — a term that exists nowhere in your files; if the reader defines it confidently, it is fabricating, and everything else it produced is suspect.

**The growth loop** turns errors into architecture: error → name the shape → write the rule into the file where that work happens → attach a check and a proof date → grade whether the rule held. The master metric is **time-to-catch**: how long a wrong claim lived before the system caught it. Systems don't become perfect; the realistic ambition is that the same class of error lives for days the first time, hours the second, minutes thereafter.

## What this is not

It is not a productivity multiplier, an income system, or a promise. It does not replace domain skill — a ledger will faithfully record a bad operator's bad calls and grade them WRONG with perfect accuracy. It does not run itself; budget roughly 20–30 minutes a week for a small system. And it does not fix a dishonest operator: every mechanism here can be gamed by the person keeping the records. The method assumes the operator would rather be accurate than comfortable.

The instance this method was distilled from is never published — its figures, people, and records stay private. What is published is the method, complete enough to build your own instance from a standing start. We do not ask you to trust the private instance's results; there are no results here to trust. Run it on your own work, keep your own ledger, and grade the claims against your own receipts.
